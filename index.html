      How the 64 profiles and place suggestions work

      Profile scheme: 16 familiar four-letter types × Assertive (A) / Turbulent (T) × Cooperative (C) / Self-directed (S). AC, AS, TC and TS are this guide’s four variants. These extra axes are an app-specific planning framework, not an official MBTI assessment or a standardized 64-type taxonomy.

      Interpretation: Each base profile has separate living and working preference weights across nine environmental factors. Variant weights adjust the emphasis on routine, collaboration, focus and autonomy. They describe optional preferences to reflect on, not ability or fixed traits. Any person can thrive outside these suggestions.

      Ranking: Each named district has editorial living and working ratings from 1 to 5. The score is the sum of each rating × its profile weight, divided by the sum of weights. Results show ordinal suggestions, not scientific fit percentages. Ties are ordered by place name. Remote mode uses living ratings for the work base and reduces the emphasis on local technical and business opportunities; local-workplace mode excludes Nuvali and Copenhagen, for which this guide does not supply a documented local employment ecosystem.

      Evidence boundary: The linked primary sources describe local amenities or ecosystems. The personality-to-place mapping and the numeric ratings are our planning assumptions, not findings from those sources. The list is curated and does not prove a globally “best” location. Geography does not define the personalities of residents.

      CliftonStrengths tagging: All 34 themes and their four domain groupings follow Gallup’s official reference. Enter a ranked Top 5 or a longer list before applying your themes. Saving the top-strengths profile leaves place results in place; Apply my top strengths saves it and applies its themes using the selected domain and match mode. Other theme and domain edits also wait for Apply CliftonStrengths filters. Clearing filters preserves your saved ranked profile. Rank is recorded for reference, not a scoring weight. Place activity tags are independently selected planning labels, with separate lists for living and working setups. Match any includes a setup with at least one selected theme; Match all requires every selected theme. A domain filter also requires a tag from that domain. Filters narrow candidate places before the personality ranking; they do not change your type or infer your actual strengths. No match is shown explicitly rather than replacing your filters. Domain-only filtering works without selecting themes. CliftonStrengths and Gallup are trademarks of Gallup, Inc.; this independent guide is not affiliated with or endorsed by Gallup.

      Before choosing: Compare your actual offer, total household budget, family and community ties, address-specific safety and hazards, accessibility, language, and commute. For another country, establish your right to live and work there. A job search link is a search, not a verified vacancy or an eligibility check.

        🔎 How this was built

        Five subagents ran in parallel, each on one data source, returning only signals (places, work clues, lifestyle) — never raw private content.

        🏗️ Property prices & monthly payments

        The Properties view imports the qualified shortlist from PH Property Value Finder and shows asking price, estimated actual value, recommended down payment, loan needed, expected rent, and monthly cash flow together.

        The live property screen uses the source assessment's 20-year term and 6.5% baseline; recommended down payment can vary by deal. These are screening estimates, not bank quotes or professional valuations. The older place cards retain their original illustrative project comparisons for broader location research.

        How scoring works

        Every candidate is rated 0–100 on each criterion (seeded from your signals). Your priority weights produce a weighted average. Change a weight and the weighted average — and the ranking — moves. No candidate is "objectively" best; the ranking is best fit for what you said matters.

        Compass is a thinking aid, not an oracle. It makes your trade-offs explicit. The final call is yours.
